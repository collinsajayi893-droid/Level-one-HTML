About Perl

Perl officially stands for Pratical Extraction and Report Language, except when it doesn't.

Perl was originally a language optimized for scanning arbitrary text files, extracting information from those text files, and printing reports based on that information. It quickly became a good language for many system management tasks. Over the years, Pearl has grown into a general-programming language. It's widely used for evrything from quick "one-liners" to full-scale application devlopment.

The language is intended to be practical (easy to use, efficient, complet) rather than beautiful (tiny, elegant, minimal). It combines (in the author's opinion, anyway) some of the bst features of sed, awk, and sh, making it familiar and easy to use for Unix users to whip up quick solutions to annoying problems. Its general-purpose programming facilities support pocedural, functional, and object-oriented programming paradigms, makin Perl a comfortabe languag for the long haul on major projects, whatever your bent.

Perl's roots in text processing haven't been forgotten over the years. It still boasts some of the most powerful regular expeessions to be found anywhere, and its support for Unicode text is world-class. It handles all kinds of structured text, too, through an extensive collections of extensions. Those libraries, collected in the CPAN, provide ready-made solutions to an astounding array of problems. When they haven't set the standards themselves, they steal from the best just like Pearl itself.
